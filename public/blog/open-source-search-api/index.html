Why We Built an Open Source Search API (And Why MIT Matters)

By Adarsh Kushwah • September 3, 2026 • 6 min read

In the rapid rush to build generative AI systems, access to fresh, structured web information has become the ultimate bottleneck. If your LLM cannot read what happened an hour ago or retrieve live technical documentation, it is trapped in the static memory of its training weights.

Unfortunately, the search infrastructure layer has consolidated around two extremes: expensive, opaque SaaS platforms with aggressive metering, and "open-source" tools licensed under restrictive copyleft licenses like the AGPL-3.0.

The AGPL Trap in AI Infrastructure

Many modern scraping tools market themselves as "open source," but apply the GNU Affero General Public License (AGPL). For hobbyists, this distinction rarely causes concern. For commercial startups and enterprises, however, AGPL presents an existential risk:

  • Network Copyleft: If you modify an AGPL component and offer a service over a network, you are legally obligated to release your complete proprietary application source code to the public.
  • Legal Department Bans: Nearly all Fortune 500 engineering organizations and venture-backed tech companies outright prohibit AGPL dependencies in their production stacks.

Why Jiro is 100% MIT Licensed

We chose the MIT License for Jiro deliberately and without compromise. Under MIT:

  • You can embed Jiro inside commercial closed-source SaaS products.
  • You can modify the engine code to support proprietary internal data sources without publishing your changes.
  • You can package Jiro with hardware appliances or on-prem enterprise deployments without legal ambiguity.
Our Philosophy: True developer infrastructure should empower engineers to build businesses, not create legal landmines or force companies onto proprietary managed cloud tiers.

Data Sovereignty & Privacy

When an enterprise queries a third-party search API, every internal research prompt, competitor investigation, or confidential customer query is transmitted to an external logging database. In regulated industries (finance, healthcare, legal), this is a compliance violation.

With Jiro, your search queries never leave your private VPC. You can run Jiro on an isolated air-gapped subnet or a local developer machine, with zero analytics, zero phone-home pings, and zero third-party telemetry.
